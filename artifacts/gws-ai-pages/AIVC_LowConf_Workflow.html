How each line's daily result reaches Google Drive, Data Studio and Google Chat

Every night at 12:15 am each AIVC PC counts yesterday's low-confidence images, saves the result on its D drive and uploads it to one shared Google Drive folder and one Google Sheet. Every morning at 8 am a check posts to Google Chat whether all lines arrived. A new .bat is released once in Drive and every PC updates itself.

Every night, on each PC

12:15 am · Task Scheduler

  1. All day, AIVC saves each low-confidence image and its .txt label in D:\AIVCdata\tag_low_conf_….
  2. At 12:15 am Task Scheduler starts daily_low_conf_result.bat as AIVCDeleteBot. No window opens.
  3. The .bat finds the AIVC folder in use on C:, whatever its name, and reads factory and line, the class names and the token from it.
  4. It counts yesterday's images per station and side, and the classes from the labels. A day of the last 7 that has images but no result yet is counted too.
  5. It saves ResultDailyLowConfidence_RASM_<date>.csv and …_FKTH_<date>.csv in D:\AIVCdata\ResultDailyLowConfidence\.
  6. After a random wait of 0–3 minutes it uploads them, trying up to 5 times. The receiver saves the files in Drive and the rows in the Sheet; a day sent again replaces its old rows.
  7. It reports its status to the Sheet's Status tab. If a newer .bat is waiting in _update, it downloads it and replaces itself, keeping this PC's settings.

Every morning, in Google

8:00 am · Apps Script

  1. The daily check reads the Data and Status tabs.
  2. It posts one message to the Google Chat space: “OK: all lines uploaded”, or the lines that didn't upload yesterday and the PCs whose last run failed.
  3. PCs still on an older .bat are listed under the message for information.

Releasing a new .bat

once · by you

  1. Check that LC_VERSION in the new file is higher than the one on the PCs.
  2. Upload it to AIVC Low Confidence/_update/ as daily_low_conf_result.bat (or .txt), replacing the old one.
  3. Each PC takes it at its next run and keeps the old file as .bak.
  4. The Status tab's “.bat version” column shows which PCs have it.
